10 — Notifications · the ask and the bell

Pip's whole promise is that most days nothing needs you — so a notification has to be rare enough to mean something. Two channels only: a push alert when a pot crosses a line, and a Sunday email that summarises the week. Both are asked for once, in Pip's own sheet, before the operating system ever gets involved, and both can be turned off from the same bell you read them in.

Why the switches live in the panel

The moment you want notifications off is the moment one annoys you — and that moment happens inside the list, not three taps deep in Settings. So the two switches sit at the foot of the panel, under the thing they govern. Settings mirrors them; neither is the “real” one.

Turning push off never clears the list. The bell is the archive, always readable, with or without permission — so switching off costs you nothing you'd miss.

What earns a notification
YesA pot crosses its target or cap — the only alert that ever mentions doing something, and it says what your broker already did. YesA connection breaks or a feed is over six hours quiet. YesA round-number milestone, first time only. Off by default for the jumpy. NoDaily price moves, in either direction, however large. 11 — Net assets · the number that sets the ceiling

Side Bet can't exceed 10% of everything you're worth — that's the rule Pip has to work to, not a preference. Which means Pip needs one figure it has no other way of knowing: your net assets, house and pension and savings included, most of which live nowhere near a broker. It's the single most sensitive number in the app and the one you need to see least often, so it's entered behind dots and stays behind dots.

Why dots
Your portfolio total is yours and it's on the home screen because you want it there. Your net worth is a different thing — it's the number that identifies you on a train, and you never need it at a glance. So it behaves like a password: masked on entry, masked on the row, revealed only while you hold the eye open.
Asked at the end of first login, never the start — nobody types their net worth into an app that hasn't shown them anything yet.
One figure, one purpose. Pip never uses it to size a suggestion, rank you, or unlock anything.
